# PIPEDA — meaningful consent

## Summary

Under PIPEDA, the knowledge and consent of the individual are required for the collection, use, or disclosure of personal information, except where the law says consent is not appropriate. Consent is only meaningful if the person understands what they are agreeing to.

The OPC's guidance on obtaining meaningful consent expects organizations to explain, in plain language, what personal information they collect, why they collect it, who it will be shared with, and what the risks are. Purposes must be identified before or at the time of collection, and stated so the individual can reasonably understand them.

The form of consent depends on the sensitivity of the information and the reasonable expectations of the person. Express consent is generally expected for sensitive information such as health or financial data. Implied consent may be acceptable for less sensitive information when the person would reasonably expect the use. Consent must not be made a condition of supplying a product or service unless the collection is necessary for that purpose, and a person can withdraw consent at any time, subject to legal or contractual restrictions.
