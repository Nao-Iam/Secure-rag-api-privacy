# PIPEDA — individual access and challenging compliance

## Summary

Two of PIPEDA's ten fair information principles give individuals rights over their own personal information: individual access and challenging compliance.

Under the individual access principle, an individual has the right to be told whether an organization holds personal information about them, to be given access to that information on request, and to challenge its accuracy and completeness and have it amended where appropriate. Organizations must generally respond to an access request within 30 days, with extensions allowed only in limited circumstances.

Under the challenging compliance principle, an individual can challenge an organization's compliance with the ten principles. The challenge should first be directed to the individual the organization has designated as accountable for its privacy compliance. If the matter is not resolved, the individual can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, which investigates complaints and can take matters to the Federal Court.
