# Government of Canada — Guide on the use of generative AI

- Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/responsible-use-ai/generative-ai/guide-use-generative-ai.html
- Accessed: 2026-11-11
- Note: Summary — paraphrased, not a verbatim quote.
- Trust tier: 1 — trusted (official guidance summary)

## Summary

The Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat's Guide on the use of generative AI gives guidance to federal institutions on using generative AI tools. It says institutions should explore potential uses, but should not use these tools in all cases: they must be cautious, evaluate the risks before they start using them, and limit use to cases where they can manage the risks effectively.

Before using a generative AI tool, federal institutions must assess and mitigate ethical, legal, and other risks, including privacy, security, intellectual property, and human rights risks. They should engage stakeholders such as legal counsel and privacy and security experts before deploying a tool for public use or for service delivery.

The guide recommends using anonymized or de-identified information in prompts where possible, entering personal information only where authorized, telling affected people when a generative AI tool will be used, checking outputs for accuracy, and keeping accountability for decisions with the organization, not the tool. It also warns that public chatbots are a higher-risk use case, and that institutions should consider privacy impact assessments and retention schedules for prompts and outputs.
