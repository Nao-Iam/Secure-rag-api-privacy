# OPC — principles for responsible, trustworthy, privacy-protective generative AI

- Source: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/
- Accessed: 2026-11-11
- Note: Summary — paraphrased, not a verbatim quote.
- Trust tier: 1 — trusted (official guidance summary)

## Summary

On December 7, 2023, the federal, provincial, and territorial privacy regulators jointly published "Principles for responsible, trustworthy and privacy-protective generative AI technologies". The document explains how key Canadian privacy principles apply to generative AI. It is guidance, not law, but it shows how regulators read the existing laws.

Its principles include: legal authority and consent — organizations must have legal authority to collect and use personal information, and consent, where relied on, must be valid and meaningful; appropriate purposes — generative AI should only be used for purposes a reasonable person would consider appropriate; necessity and proportionality — use anonymized, synthetic, or de-identified data rather than personal information where personal information is not required; openness and transparency about how information is used and the privacy risks involved; accountability, including making AI tools explainable to users; and safeguards, including limiting the sharing of personal, sensitive, or confidential information.

The principles also note that outputs about an individual from a generative AI system are still personal information, and that developers and providers should inform users about known limitations in the accuracy of generative AI outputs.
