## Summary

The Treasury Board Directive on Automated Decision-Making took effect on April 1, 2019. It applies to federal institutions using an automated decision system — any technology that assists or replaces the judgment of a human decision-maker — to make or support administrative decisions about clients. Its objective is to ensure automated decision systems are deployed in a way that reduces risks to Canadians and leads to more efficient, accurate, consistent, and interpretable decisions.

Before an automated decision system goes into production, the institution must complete an Algorithmic Impact Assessment. The assessment scores the system's impact from Level I (lowest) to Level IV (highest), based on factors such as the impact on the rights of individuals or communities, privacy, health, and economic interests, and whether the impact is reversible. The final results of the assessment must be publicly released.

Requirements then scale with the assessed impact level: peer review of the system, a Gender-based Analysis Plus, plain-language notice that a decision was made in whole or in part by an automated decision system, a meaningful explanation of how and why the decision was made, human intervention where appropriate, employee training, contingency planning, and approval to operate. All systems must also be tested and monitored for unintended data biases before production.
